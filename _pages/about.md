I am a fast-track Ph.D. candidate in Neuroscience at the [Graduate School of Systemic Neurosciences](https://www.gsn.uni-muenchen.de/) at LMU Munich, working with Professor Wiktor Młynarski. I explore sensory systems and behavior through the lens of natural scene statistics, efficient coding, information theory, and population coding.

Research
======
My Ph.D. research investigates how behavior modulates sensory inputs and therefore processing. Currently, I am exploring how animals build joint sensory-behavioral representations to estimate the state of the world while they move. Previously, by extending efficient coding frameworks to account for how locomotion modulates visual inputs, I developed a normative theory that explains why neural coding in the visual systems of diverse species, from rodents to primates, changes during locomotion. This work was recently published in [*Science Advances*](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aed4172) and received the COSYNE PRX Life Talk Award in 2025.

Background
======
I received a B.S. in Physics and a B.S. in Mathematics from the University of Florida in 2022, where I was a Stamps Scholar and received the Sawyer Undergraduate Thesis Award. As an undergraduate I worked with Professor Thomas Mareci at the McKnight Brain Institute on brain structural networks, building the Diffusion Image Network Simulator (DINS), and as a visiting student researcher with Dr. Arturo Deza and Professor Tomaso Poggio at the MIT Center for Brains, Minds, and Machines on biologically inspired mechanisms for adversarial robustness in CNNs. Before joining the Młynarski lab, I studied synaptic plasticity and excitation-inhibition balance in dendritic spiking networks with Professor Julijana Gjorgjieva at the Technical University of Munich.

Outside of research, I enjoy science outreach and community building. I currently serve on the organizing committee of Talking Science in Munich and have previously coordinated NeuroCamp, a neuroscience summer school for high school students.
