### Joint sensory-behavioral representations

Animals move constantly, and every movement of the head, eyes and body changes what reaches the senses. I am developing theories of how the brain combines sensory input with signals about its own behavior to build joint representations that estimate the state of the surrounding environment during active behavior. A central question is how head and eye movements reshape visual input, and what this means for efficient sensory codes.

### Efficient coding during locomotion

Neural responses in the visual system change when an animal runs, from rodents to primates. By extending the efficient coding hypothesis to account for how locomotion alters the statistics of visual input, I developed a normative explanation for these changes: they are what an optimal visual system should do when the body is in motion. This work was published in [*Science Advances*](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aed4172) and received the COSYNE PRX Life Talk Award in 2025.

### Earlier work

Before my Ph.D., I studied how synaptic plasticity shapes excitation-inhibition balance in dendritic spiking networks, how biologically inspired foveation improves the adversarial robustness of convolutional neural networks, and how structural brain networks can be simulated and analyzed from diffusion MRI.
